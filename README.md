**Study Site:** Field transect surveys were conducted in 2026 across oak stands (*Quercus* sp.) at the Middle East Technical University (METU) Campus in Ankara, Turkey[cite: 8].

### 1. Distance-Decay Dispersal Kernel
Standardizing counts by expanding sector area reveals a clear distance-decay pattern, peaking within the $0\text{–}5\text{ m}$ zone and declining steadily toward $50\text{ m}$.

### 2. Canopy Cover Selection ($\chi^2 = 352.52, df = 2, p = 2.83 \times 10^{-77}$)
Sapling recruitment strongly favors closed forest canopy (`Observed = 655` vs. `Area-Expected = 406.8`) while being severely limited in open habitats (`Observed = 18` vs. `Area-Expected = 170.6`).

### 3. Slope Orientation Effects ($\chi^2 = 98.94, df = 2, p = 3.28 \times 10^{-22}$)
Topographic slope significantly shapes recruitment across distance bands compared to flat terrain (`Observed = 113` vs. `Area-Expected = 232.8`).
